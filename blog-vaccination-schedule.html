Child Vaccination Schedule (Birth to 5 Years)

A complete, doctor-approved guide to child vaccination schedule in India covering essential vaccines, benefits, safety, side effects, and expert pediatric care. Learn the recommended baby vaccines timeline from birth to 5 years and keep your child protected through timely immunization.

Introduction

Vaccination plays a vital role in protecting children from dangerous and preventable diseases during the most sensitive years of life. A proper child vaccination schedule helps build immunity step by step from birth through early childhood.

Newborn babies have developing immune systems, which means they need early protection against infections. Timely baby vaccines help the body recognize harmful viruses and bacteria before they cause serious illness.

From birth to 5 years, vaccines protect children from illnesses such as polio, hepatitis B, tuberculosis, measles, rubella, diphtheria, tetanus, whooping cough, pneumonia, and more.

Why Vaccination is Important

  • Protects children from serious and life-threatening diseases
  • Builds a stronger immune system naturally
  • Prevents spread of infections in schools and communities
  • Reduces hospitalization and emergency treatment risk
  • Supports healthy physical and mental development
  • Helps children stay active and healthy
  • Protects family members including elderly grandparents

Complete Vaccination Schedule (0–5 Years)

At Birth

  • BCG – Protection against tuberculosis
  • OPV – Early protection from polio
  • Hepatitis B – Protects liver from viral infection

6 Weeks

  • DTP – Diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis
  • IPV – Injectable polio vaccine
  • Hib – Prevents meningitis and severe infections
  • Rotavirus – Prevents severe diarrhea
  • PCV – Protection from pneumonia

10 Weeks

  • Second doses of DTP, IPV, Hib, Rotavirus

14 Weeks

  • Third doses of DTP, IPV, Hib, Rotavirus

6 Months

  • Hepatitis B completion dose
  • Influenza vaccine (if advised)

9–12 Months

  • MMR – Measles, mumps, rubella
  • Typhoid vaccine (if advised)

12–18 Months

  • MMR booster
  • Chickenpox vaccine
  • Hepatitis A vaccine
  • DTP booster

4–5 Years

  • Final booster doses of DTP, OPV/IPV, and MMR

Note: Vaccine schedules may vary based on doctor advice, health condition, and updated national guidelines.

Benefits of Timely Vaccination

Following the correct child vaccination schedule provides stronger protection because vaccines work best when given at the recommended age.

  • Strong immunity during early childhood
  • Lower risk of severe complications
  • Better protection before school admission
  • Less chance of missing important doses
  • Reduced medical expenses from preventable diseases
  • Confidence and peace of mind for parents

Side Effects (Normal & Temporary)

Most vaccines are safe and side effects are mild. These reactions usually show the body is responding normally.

  • Mild fever
  • Pain or swelling at injection site
  • Temporary irritability
  • Reduced appetite
  • Sleepiness or tiredness
  • Mild rash after some vaccines

These effects usually disappear within 24–48 hours. If symptoms continue, contact your doctor.

When to See a Doctor

  • High fever above 102°F
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Severe allergic reaction
  • Continuous crying for many hours
  • Seizures or unusual body movements
  • Extreme weakness or dehydration

Tips for Parents Before Vaccination

  • Carry previous vaccination records
  • Dress child in comfortable clothes
  • Feed baby normally unless advised otherwise
  • Inform doctor about fever or allergies
  • Stay for observation after vaccination
  • Ask next due date before leaving hospital

FAQ

Is vaccination mandatory?
Vaccination is strongly recommended to protect children from preventable diseases.

Can vaccines be delayed?
Delays are not recommended unless advised by a pediatrician.

Are vaccines safe?
Yes, approved vaccines are tested for safety and effectiveness.

Do all children need same vaccines?
Most children follow a standard schedule, but doctors may customize if needed.

What if I missed a dose?
Consult your doctor for a catch-up vaccination plan.
