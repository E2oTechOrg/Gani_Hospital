Child Vaccination Schedule

A complete guide to your child’s vaccination schedule from birth to 5 years to ensure strong immunity and protection.

Updated April 2026 • 7 min read

Introduction

A proper child vaccination schedule is essential to protect your child from serious and life-threatening diseases. Vaccines help build strong immunity from birth and ensure healthy growth during the early years of life.

From birth to 5 years, your child requires multiple vaccines at specific intervals. Following the correct immunization schedule for babies ensures timely protection against infections like polio, measles, hepatitis, and more.

Why Vaccination is Important for Children

Vaccination is one of the most effective ways to prevent diseases. It not only protects your child but also helps protect the community by reducing the spread of infections.

  • Prevents serious diseases like measles, polio, and tuberculosis
  • Strengthens immune system naturally
  • Reduces hospitalization and medical costs
  • Ensures long-term health and development

Complete Child Vaccination Schedule (0 to 5 Years)

Below is a general baby vaccination chart followed by most pediatricians. Always consult your doctor for a personalized schedule.

At Birth

  • BCG – protects against tuberculosis
  • OPV – protects against polio
  • Hepatitis B – protects liver from infection

6 Weeks

  • DTP – protects against diphtheria, tetanus, and pertussis
  • IPV – polio protection
  • Hib – prevents brain infections
  • Rotavirus – protects against diarrhea

10 Weeks

  • Second dose of DTP
  • IPV and Hib continuation

14 Weeks

  • Third dose of DTP
  • Final primary doses of IPV and Hib

6 Months

  • Hepatitis B final dose
  • Influenza vaccine (recommended yearly)

9 Months

  • MMR (Measles, Mumps, Rubella)

12–15 Months

  • MMR booster
  • Chickenpox vaccine
  • Hepatitis A

16–24 Months

  • DTP booster
  • Polio booster

4–5 Years

  • Final DTP booster
  • MMR booster

Care Tips After Vaccination

After vaccination, it is normal for children to experience mild discomfort. Proper care helps reduce side effects and keeps your child comfortable.

  • Apply cold compress to reduce swelling
  • Give plenty of fluids
  • Allow rest and sleep
  • Follow doctor’s advice for fever medication

Common Side Effects of Vaccines

Most vaccine side effects are mild and temporary.

  • Low-grade fever
  • Pain or redness at injection site
  • Irritability or crying
  • Loss of appetite

These symptoms usually disappear within 24–48 hours.

When to See a Doctor

Although rare, some reactions need medical attention:

  • High fever above 102°F
  • Severe swelling or redness
  • Persistent crying for hours
  • Allergic reactions (rash, breathing difficulty)

Tips for Parents

  • Maintain a vaccination record card
  • Never skip scheduled doses
  • Consult pediatrician regularly
  • Follow proper nutrition and hygiene

Vaccination Schedule in India

In India, the vaccination schedule is recommended by pediatricians based on guidelines from WHO and the Government of India. Parents should ensure timely vaccination as per the national immunization program.

Private hospitals may include additional vaccines like pneumococcal and rotavirus for better protection.

Conclusion

Following a proper child vaccination schedule is one of the best ways to ensure your child grows up healthy and protected from diseases. Timely immunization, proper care, and regular checkups will give your child a strong foundation for life.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is vaccination safe for children?
Yes, vaccines are tested and safe.

Can vaccines be delayed?
It is not recommended. Follow the schedule strictly.

Are side effects normal?
Yes, mild side effects are common and temporary.

Do vaccines protect for life?
Some require booster doses for long-term protection.
