Essential Prenatal Checkups: Tests & Scans You Should Not Skip

Updated April 2026 • 5 min read

Introduction

Prenatal checkups are essential for monitoring the health of both the mother and the baby during pregnancy. These regular medical visits help doctors track fetal development, identify potential risks early, and ensure a safe and healthy pregnancy journey.

From early blood tests to advanced ultrasound scans, each checkup plays a crucial role in preventing complications and providing peace of mind to expecting mothers. Understanding these tests helps you stay prepared and confident throughout your pregnancy.

Why Prenatal Checkups Are Important

These visits allow doctors to track baby development, manage maternal health, and prevent complications. Routine tests and scans provide critical insights into fetal growth and overall pregnancy progress.

First Trimester Tests (Weeks 1–12)

  • Blood Tests: Check blood group, anemia, infections
  • Urine Test: Detect sugar, protein, infections
  • Dating Ultrasound: Confirms pregnancy and due date
  • Thyroid Test: Ensures hormonal balance

Second Trimester Tests (Weeks 13–26)

  • Anomaly Scan: Detailed scan to check baby’s organs
  • Glucose Screening: Detects gestational diabetes
  • Quadruple Marker Test: Screens for genetic conditions

Third Trimester Tests (Weeks 27–40)

  • Growth Scan: Monitors baby size and position
  • Non-Stress Test (NST): Checks baby’s heart rate
  • Blood Pressure Monitoring: Detects preeclampsia

How Often Should You Visit the Doctor?

  • Every 4 weeks (early pregnancy)
  • Every 2 weeks (after 28 weeks)
  • Weekly visits (after 36 weeks)

Important Tips for Prenatal Care

  • Never skip scheduled checkups
  • Maintain a healthy diet
  • Take prescribed supplements
  • Report unusual symptoms immediately

Frequently Asked Questions

How many prenatal visits are required?
Typically 8–10 visits are recommended for a healthy pregnancy.

Are ultrasound scans safe?
Yes, ultrasound scans are safe and commonly used to monitor baby development.

Can I skip tests if I feel healthy?
No, some conditions show no symptoms and can only be detected through tests.
